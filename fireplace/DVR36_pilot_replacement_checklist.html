Pilot Assembly Replacement Checklist

Majestic DVR36 (36" rear-vent, installed 2000) • Vermont Castings SIT Pilot Assembly LP, SRV10002265 • Date:
Gas smell at any point: leave the house, call the propane supplier or 911 from outside, touch no switches. Your unit's manual and labels override this sheet.

0. Verify before you start

  • Rating plate says LP (propane) — the part is LP only
  • Gas valve brand: □ SIT   □ Honeywell   (Honeywell: compare old and new pilot closely before disassembly; SRV10002265 is the SIT 3-way for pilots with thermocouple + thermopile)
  • Part number on old pilot / valve:
  • Kit contents present: pilot hood with tubing, thermocouple, thermopile, igniter electrode and wire
  • New pilot matches old: tube length, bracket holes, fitting sizes
  • Tools: 3/8" & 7/16" wrenches, small adjustable wrench, nut drivers, screwdrivers, multimeter (mV DC), shop vac with soft brush, flashlight, gloves, leak solution, camera, fresh batteries

1. Shut down

  • Gas supply valve OFF
  • Unit cooled at least 1 hour; control knob OFF
  • Room ventilated; no flames or sparks nearby

2. Access

  • Photograph the firebox, logs, wiring and tubing before touching anything
  • Remove glass door assembly; store it safely
  • Remove logs and embers (mark positions)
  • Remove burner / panels as needed to reach pilot and valve
  • Photograph pilot tube, thermocouple, thermopile and igniter wire runs

3. Remove old assembly

  • Disconnect pilot gas tube at valve (two wrenches; don't twist the tubing)
  • Disconnect thermocouple at valve
  • Disconnect thermopile leads
  • Disconnect igniter wire
  • Remove pilot bracket screws/nut and slide out the assembly
  • Compare old vs. new side by side

4. Install new assembly

  • Mount bracket in the same position and orientation as the old one
  • Route pilot tube with gentle curves — no kinks or sharp bends
  • Connect pilot gas tube: hand-tight, then snug with wrench
  • Connect thermocouple: finger-tight plus about 1/4 turn (check SIT sheet in the kit)
  • Connect thermopile leads
  • Connect igniter wire; keep it clear of hot surfaces
  • Compare all wiring with your photos

5. Maintenance while it's open

  • Vacuum burner compartment, valve area and pilot hood
  • Clean burner ports; check logs for soot or damage
  • Inspect door gasket for cracks, gaps or hardening (direct-vent seal)
  • Clean glass inside and out (cold, non-abrasive cleaner)
  • Inspect valve, flex line and fittings for corrosion
  • Look for rust or moisture inside the firebox
  • Check remote / wall switch and replace batteries
  • Outside: check vent termination for nests or debris; vent connections tight and sealed

6. Leak test (no flame)

  • Open gas supply
  • Soap/leak solution on pilot tube fitting, valve inlet and every connection touched
  • No growing bubbles. If any: gas off, tighten, retest

7. Light and verify

  • Follow the lighting instructions on the unit's label (PILOT, press and hold, ignite)
  • Hold knob about 1 minute after the pilot lights; release; pilot stays lit
  • Pilot flame steady, blue, enveloping thermocouple tip
  • Main burner lights; flame height and color normal
  • Re-check fittings for leaks with flame on
  • Replace logs per photos, then glass door (all latches secure)
  • Run 15–20 minutes; re-check for leaks and verify pilot stays lit

8. If the pilot still drops out

  • Thermocouple connection loose or over-tightened; tip not in flame
  • Pilot tube kinked or orifice blocked
  • Vent blockage or door seal leak causing drafts
  • Valve failing or wrong gas pressure: call a licensed technician
